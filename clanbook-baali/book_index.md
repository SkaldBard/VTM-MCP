**Page numbers: this index cites the book's *printed* page numbers. The

**This PDF is a scan with no text layer.** Every chunk was produced by

## Overview
A 1998 *Vampire: The Dark Ages* "black" clanbook (written by Sven Skoog
and Lucien Soulban; developed by Justin Achilli and Richard E. Dansky)
on the Baali, the infernalist bloodline built around the secret names of
sleeping pre-human "Children" of the night. It is deliberately told in
plural, biased voices — Chapter Two's opening epigraph says the narrator is
"one and all Baali… because no one voice speaks the entire truth" — and
sets out (1) the Baali's origin and multi-millennial history from the first
tribe's well in Ashur through Carthage, Rome, the plagues of the 6th
century, the Assamite war, and the rise of Azaneal at Chorazin; (2) how the
bloodline is organized in the early 13th century (orders, covens, the
"Ba'al" pantheon, the D'habi revenants, Roads, Merits/Flaws and Daimoinon
powers); and (3) seven character templates plus a gallery of infamous
Baali. Its present-day setting is the early 13th century (it references the
courts of Philip of Swabia and Pope Innocent III). It carries a content
note (p.2): intended for readers over 18; the views expressed belong to the
in-character narrators, not the publisher. The two bookend fiction pieces
contain graphic sexual violence and torture.

**Which game line this is:** the *original, classic Dark Ages line* (1998),
using classic Storyteller-System Dark Ages rules — *not* V20. It refers the
reader to the original *Vampire: The Dark Ages* core book, *Dark Ages
Companion*, *Libellus Sanguinis I: Masters of the State* and *Constantinople
by Night*. **Do not confuse its "Dark Ages Companion" with this library's
`v20-dark-ages-companion`** — they are different books. See
`library_index.md` for how this book's account of the Baali compares with
the V20 and classic modern-nights treatments.

## Chapter One: The Opening (printed pp. 7-10; DB pp. 8-11)
**Summary:** A fiction piece, not analysis. Jonathan, an abbot, has taken a
beautiful boy as a lover; the boy is a vampire-thing whose body hides a
swarm. The swarm enters Jonathan during intercourse, the creature keeps him
captive in darkness for a fortnight-plus of torture and hallucination,
sews ancient patterns into his flesh with a silk thread soaked in potent
vitae (the wounds close at once), tells him he will be needed "later," and
explains "what you should know before apotheosis." The thread resumes as
the "Endgame" sidebar at the end of Chapter Two (p.27), where flies
emerge from Jonathan's body and the watcher says it is "time to awaken the
darkness." Printed p.10 (DB p.11) is a full-page illustration.
**Key themes:** the swarm/insect motif that recurs through the book
(Avatars of the Swarm, Brood Mothers, Lord of Flies); consecration through
desecration; the Embrace as horror rather than gift.
**Key terms/entities:** Jonathan (abbot), the fey child (a Baali of the
Swarm tradition), the silk thread and "ancient patterns" (p.9).

## Chapter Two: Dissonant Echoes (printed pp. 11-28; DB pp. 12-29)
Told as a plural, unreliable history; it is the book's core lore chapter.
Epigraph: "The voice you hear is one and all Baali… no one voice speaks the
entire truth." — The Voice of Truth (p.11).

### "Eight in Eternity: A Fragment of Baali History" (p.12)
**Summary:** A numerological prophecy, "excerpts from the Distrustful
Dreams of Angra Mainyu the Deceiver, High Priest of Baal," addressed to the
narrator's "beloved." Each number is paired with an event or rival that
shaped the Baali — First (the unnamed Ancestor, the "Dread Lord"), Second
(Crete, the Minotaur and Ariadne's thread, "our enemy… whose armies unmade
our unity in Crete"), Tripartite (Egypt/the Setites and their cult of child-
kings), Four (the Carpenter/Christ, "our greatest adversary"), Six (a
"ragged shepherd-king" ruling twelve tribes), Seventh (Mohammed, "the one who
submits"), Eighth (Zarathustra and eight-pointed circles), and Five (the five
senses/elements/"Caine's five childer"/ages of man — "the inconsistency").
It closes "Infinity. Our destiny."
**Key themes:** the Baali read all later religions as pale reflections of
their own cosmology; the "inconsistency" as the hinge of the Baali world.
**Note:** attributed to a named, self-interested in-character voice, not the
book's neutral narrator.

### "The First Tribe" and "Wells of Sacrifice" (pp.13-16)
**Summary:** The Baali cosmogony. The sky-god's light cast the "Children"
of Night down like falling stars; survivors burrowed into the earth and
slept while humanity unknowingly built cities over them. A mortal band —
"the first tribe" — digging a well outside Ashur uncovered one Child, which
died screaming, its flesh rising as poisoned smoke; its words carried the
first tribe forever after. They learned the Children's true names and made
themselves acolytes. Naming a Child pulls it nearer to waking, so the first
tribe hid the names inside their own heads and kept the Children asleep
with a "murderer's lullaby" of sacrificial atrocity. Later cult priests
changed or masked names behind foreign languages, drawing on a fraction of
the power; the narrator says Mesopotamian "gods" were distorted
memories of the sleeping, demoniac Children. In Ashur, a sacrificial
cult fed an organ pit/well for generations until a Cainite "of great power
and majesty" destroyed the priests and let his vitae flow into the well;
three mortals who survived lapped it up and rose the next night as the first
Baali.
**Key themes:** names as power and contagion; sacrifice as sedation; the
Baali as an inherited, not invented, cult.
**Key terms/entities:** the Children/Sleepers, the first tribe, Ashur,
the well/organ pit, true names (and the narrator's refusal to give them,
p.14), the Cainite stranger (unnamed, p.16).

### "The Three" and "Ashur the Unnamed" (pp.16-18; the third Baali's discussion runs into p.18)
**Summary:** The three first Baali, "each equal in power," came out of the
well: **Nergal** (the legend behind the Babylonian deity; founded
Mashkan-shapir; most adept with other Cainites; believed his sire was
Ashur; chose to Embrace outside the first tribe), **Moloch** (also
Andramelech and Ba'al Hammon; progenitor of several orders including the
Avatars of the Swarm; spread the Baali across the Phoenician Empire;
Embraced mostly from the first tribe), and a **third whose name and even
gender are unknown** (Zillah, Lilith, "the Crone," a merged pair of lovers,
or — whispered — Saulot himself; dozens of claimants such as Pazuzu,
Ahriman and Ba'al were exposed). A framed sidebar, **"Ashur the Unnamed,"**
lists mutually contradictory origin tales for the Baali's *sire*: a
Mesopotamian slave-boy turned by a wanderer who still travels with twelve
disciples; a nomad tribe caught in the service of "the fly-king" at the
crater of Chorazin; Ashur, warrior-philosopher god-king whose eastern
campaigns failed; a Cappadocian origin; a third, unsleeping eye. It
asks whether the Baali were "born of Ashur-called-Cappadocius… an unnamed
ancient from the East… gentle Saulot himself" and concludes the secret "may
lie hidden forever."
**Key themes:** deliberately unresolved origin; Saulot and Cappadocius as
rumored progenitors (see `library_index.md` for how this plays against
`clanbook-salubri`).

### "The Lords" (p.18) — what "Baali" means
**Summary:** Vampires assume "Baali" means the Canaanite deity Baal. It
does not: from *ba'al* "lord," a title used across Phoenicia (Ba'alat of
Berytus; Heracles as Ba'al of Tyre; Moloch as Ba'al Hammon at Carthage). The
bloodline name is both self-importance and a way to hide a real name (call
yourself Baali and no other title and you protect your true name), and
Mesopotamian empires used "Ba'al" to hide gods' names from enemy
"wooing." Cainites who read Baali scriptures mistook the Children for the
mortals' Baal; Ba'al became "Beelzebub, Lord of the Flies"; during the
First Crusade, Ventrue-backed Knights Templar found Avatars of the Swarm at
Tyre and tied them to Beelzebub. **Younger Baali believe their own
propaganda** and take Baal as their patron demon, widening the rift between
orders and generations.
**Key terms/entities:** Ba'al/Baal Hammon/Beelzebub, Avatars of the Swarm,
the Templars at Tyre.

### "Nergal's Legacy" (pp.18-19)
**Summary:** Moloch calls Nergal "the whore of the Baali": powerful, self-
centered, convinced of his own divinity; he coaxed secrets from other
Cainites (served Arikel, traded vices with the Tzimisce Antediluvian,
debated Troile). His city **Mashkan-shapir** on the Tigris had two harbors
and a temple a quarter of its size, staffed by the **D'habi**, a line of
ghoul-priests. Learning a Child (Namtaru, "Spreader of Plagues") slept
beneath the city but not his true name, Nergal resolved to feed it the
city's mortals with engineered plagues. His own D'habi priests told Moloch;
Moloch, wanting no Baali "civil war," betrayed Nergal to the clans. The
clans razed the city; Lasombra priests of Ereshkigal flooded the temple
with "liquid darkness," washing away Nergal and his followers. "Mashkan-
shapir died on that day."
**Notable claims:** in this telling the Baali "charade" with the clans was
a deliberate ploy; many Baali were destroyed, others dispersed to work in
seclusion.

### "Moloch and the Orphaned" (pp.20-21)
**Summary:** Moloch, quieter than Nergal, advocated subtle agendas. After
Mashkan-shapir the Baali scattered (Sidon, Tripoli, Wabar, Ashur); the
D'habi switched allegiance to the Baali as a revenant family. Someone
betrayed the names of power to other vampires and began true infernalism —
Moloch never realized it was Nergal. The **Orphaned**, Nergal's
sponsorless descendants, worshiped minor names (the roots of the Baali who
treat mortal Baal as a god). Moloch let them survive as decoys and
applauded their barbarities; Cainites destroyed the cults of Pazuzu,
Ahriman, Baal and Mot, but Moloch's authority eroded as strong Baali
defected to the Orphaned.

### "Shaitan, the Great Deceiver" and Knossos (pp.21-23)
**Summary:** Two millennia before Christ, Shaitan appears in Ashur
claiming to be the bloodline's progenitor. The Orphaned flock to him;
doubters die of a plague that turns Cainite vitae to dust. Moloch withdraws
to the Phoenician coast, having had visions of the destruction to come.
**Shaitan is Nergal**: the destruction at Mashkan-shapir, the D'habi
betrayal and his supposed death were all a ruse (a childe in his guise
vanished in the flood). He kept the D'habi Blood-Oathbound with his blood and
spied on every Baali order through them; infected rivals by poisoning their
ghouls' vitae; used a corrupt Assamite sect as assassins. As Shaitan he
built **Chorazin**, a "palace of shadows," over Namtaru's tomb north of
Galilee, then moved the desiccated Child to **Crete**, where a labyrinth
modeled on the surface of 700 mortal brains was built beneath Knossos to focus
the awakening; the sea turned blood-red. The clans united and made **Thera**
erupt; Knossos collapsed, Shaitan "reputedly died," Namtaru fell back into
fitful sleep, "too weak to awaken completely, but… no longer trapped in
dreams." Impostor "Shaitans" still surface; most Baali dismiss them as
glory-hounds, a few suspect they are stalking horses for the real one.
**Sidebar — "Desperately Seeking Satan" (p.22):** a sect of Christian-born
Baali believes Satan is already walking among society (the 1,000 years after
Christ's harrowing of Hell have passed); they commit flagrant depravity to be
noticed, and are the main reason the bloodline is thought to worship
Christian devils. **Also:** Nergal did learn his master's true name before
the end but spread only secondary, masked names to humanity and lesser
Baali to weaken Moloch's monopoly — the origin of humanity's familiarity
with names of power and of infernalism among other Cainites (p.23).

### "The Fracturing and Carthage" (p.23)
**Summary:** After Knossos the Orphaned fragment into orders around the new
monotheisms (Judaism, Mithraicism, Zoroastrianism). Moloch moves to
**Carthage**, which the Baali "claimed" as the Brujah claimed its people. The
Brujah consorted with the Baali for power, wealth or standing; the Baali
advised ghouling Hannibal's war elephants. Moloch rose as **Ba'al Hammon**,
and the Brujah Antediluvian **Troile** looked away from child sacrifices.
Moloch encouraged Troile's taste for diablerie, and the two became
Blood-Oathbound lovers. When Carthage fell in the Third Punic War, Moloch
fought beside Troile and both "melt[ed] into the ground"; Roman Ventrue and
Lasombra "salted the earth" with rituals to keep either from rising again —
and the Baali, who could have intervened, judged Moloch weak. (The
Appendix says Cybele's rituals sealed them, "where they still sleep to this
day.") The Brujah despise the Baali ever after; the narrator says the Brujah
lost their ideals not at Carthage's fall but when they sold their ethics.

### "Roman Empire & Monotheism" and "Wings of Plague" (p.24)
**Summary:** The Baali infiltrate Rome only slowly; Ventrue Methuselah
**Mithras** controlled his cult too tightly, so they attacked it from outside
by promoting Christianity, helped (unknowingly) by the Toreador **Beshter**
and the Lasombra **Montano**. After Rome falls, the "Trinity of
Methuselahs" who forged Constantinople preserves Christianity as an eastern
state faith. In the 6th century, **Hay-Tau** — a childe of Shaitan, a
**Setite thrall**, and the one who betrayed Shaitan at Knossos — stirs
Namtaru; a plague erupts from Egypt (A.D. 541), reaches Constantinople,
Britain and Ireland, spread in places by Nosferatu who control vermin. The
36 secondary names of Namtaru become the **Decani**, "36 spirits of
disease." Hay-Tau was too weak to wake Namtaru, but the Baali missed their
chance to act.

### "The Night of Bread and Parchment" (pp.24-25)
**Summary:** Islam's rise opens two fronts. **(1)** Mohammed unites the
Assamites, including infernalist assassins who worshiped Arabian demons;
Mecca falls; the unified Assamites torture those captives and sweep Arabia
Felix and the Fertile Crescent, destroying a dozen Baali nests in a week and
the Baali hold on Baghdad, Yathrib, Acre and Sidon (Baali captives are
hung upside-down in light-shielded boxes with a pinhole, a slowly moving
sunbeam cutting them in half). They leave unleavened bread as a signature.
Six strong nests meet at the **Iblii-al-Akbar** organ pit in Damascus for a
months-long ritual — two captured Assamites, 200+ mortals who swallowed
assassin blood and were flayed on weighted hooks, the blood distilled and
offered to the Decani — which **curses the Alamut Assamites**: it intensifies
their blood-lust, turning ritual Amaranth into an insatiable desire to
consume, which the Assamites disguise as philosophy. **(2)** Islam's
thirst for knowledge: Baghdad's House of Wisdom translates texts; the
Saracens learn paper-making after the Battle of Talas; Cordoba and
Catalonia's scribes spread Latin grimoires (the *Picatrix*, *Book of
Wisdom*, *Sword of Moses*, *Key of Solomon*, *Sepher Raziel*, *Lemegeton*)
across Europe; a few of the "true names" in them are accurate. The lesser Baali
"revel in this accomplishment" but it destroys the bloodline's monopoly.
**Key themes:** knowledge spreading beyond control; paper as a weapon
against secrecy; the Assamite war as the Baali's most costly defeat.

### "This Plaguey Darkness" and "An Adversary's Affairs" (pp.25-26)
**Summary:** Younger orders fight over **Chorazin**, Shaitan's former
throne, whose "lower city" is said to belong to whoever finds it. In the 11th
century **Azaneal** finds texts saying the gate is hidden in living shadows,
spends a century finding infernalist Lasombra, and allies with an **angellis
ater** ("black angels") coven in Valencia (see *Libellus Sanguinis I*). They
open the gate to a buried city built where Namtaru's body was excavated;
wards kill many coven members; Namtaru's tomb-script "leapt into the minds
of those present," shattered their souls, and set them on each other. Only
Azaneal survives, changed — he now has an inherent understanding of the
angellis ater doctrines and makes Chorazin his home, Embraces Baali loyal only
to him (all his childer prove **sterile**), and declares war on older nests
across the Levant. "This is where the Baali are now": many flee to Europe;
eldest Baali find quiet refuge or sacrifice and betray their young to local
clans; Azaneal rumored to have sent two generals into Europe.
**Notable claims:** Azaneal's Baali are not loyal to the rest of the
bloodline (p.43).

### "Endgame" (p.27)
Fiction sidebar (see Chapter One).

## Chapter Three: Descent into Darkness (printed pp. 29-48; DB pp. 30-49; printed p.48 is a full-page illustration)
Epigraph: Marlowe's *Doctor Faustus* (p.29). Opening: the Baali are "among
the least understood Cainites"; those who pry too closely are "either
destroyed by the bloodline or subsumed by it."

### Recruitment, Embrace and havens (pp.30-32)
**Summary:** Conscripts were once subverted from other clans (conversion
seen as the greatest tribute to a patron) but are now plucked carefully from
the mortal intellectual and spiritual elite (scribes, scholars,
schoolmasters); harlots, soldiers and artisans only when needed. The one
steady exception is **sincere, unflinching religious faith**, which the
Baali are drawn to corrupt. Every temple has an **organ pit** holding the
dismembered bodies and innards of victims, from "a personal bath to an
underground cistern"; the notorious ones are the Ashurite Well beneath Ashur,
the Iblii-al-Akbar beneath Damascus, and "Hell's Mouth" aboard the
**Dark Magister**, a Moorish pirate ship crewed by Baali and infernal
Lasombra corsairs, hold full of two centuries of victims and booty, last
rumored near Tripoli. **Becoming:** a sire opens his wrists into a severed
human heart, places it in the pit, drains his mortal to near death, and the
mortal must reach and eat the heart — "Embracing himself through strength of
will." **Embracing the Dead:** other-clan converts are re-Embraced by being
drained completely by a nest-master and then draining him in turn (see Merit:
Apostate, p.44). Havens are churches, temples, ruins, caverns, catacombs —
the closer to religion the better. Where Baali linger, signatures appear:
carvings, alien tongues overheard, livestock deformities, stillbirths, sour
milk, bread that won't rise.

### Clan hierarchy (pp.32-33) and sidebar "My Kiss"
**Summary:** The Baali revere the **Ba'al** ("Lords," mostly masculine
faces; female cults such as Astarte's are believed gone with Carthage's
sack) above lesser patrons (the Decani, insectile beings, "formless chaos").
Most fear even hearing a true name; many serve a Child's underlings and call
them "demons." Those allowed direct communion with a slumbering Master are
the **shaitan** ("one who sees"; later "devil"); their lieutenants are
**al'shaitan**. Below them the old formal culture has collapsed "into
fractious feudalism": scattered pockets of elders, and mostly covens of five
to a dozen under covenmasters. **Sidebar "My Kiss"** (a Brood Mother's
voice): Avatars of the Swarm alone pass on the Kiss through the swarm — the
insects need living human hosts, a 24-week hatching cycle that is always
fatal to the host, a Brood Mother seeks one to seven disciples, and two or
three brood insects carry enough vitae to ghoul a mortal or help bind a
Cainite by Blood Oath. Baali historians believe Nergal-called-Shaitan ghouled
the entire D'habi line this way.

### "Numbers of the Beast" — the Baali's rival philosophies (pp.33-37)
**Summary:** Each entry has an in-character epigraph and describes a school
of Ba'al worship:
- **The Destroyer** — "Servitors of the Void" who want everything ended so
  their masters can return (their goal at Knossos). The blood-thirsty
  cultist stereotype fits them; many have blurred into Western Satanism.
- **The Labrys** — medallion of the double axe, "horned head of Ba'al";
  worn so brethren recognize each other.
- **The Celestial** — angelic/demonic scholars (Solomonic, Gnostic,
  Qaballistic) who yoke otherworldly beings to their will and pursue godhead
  through knowledge; hard to identify; found alongside mortal and Cainite
  occultists, including Cappadocians and Tremere.
- **The Swarm** — Avatars of the Swarm: insect-hive worshippers; no concept
  of siring or generation; mortal prisoners as "birthing-flesh"; huge organ
  pits; among the most *tolerant* Baali because they unify opposites.
- **The Mystery** — the "Children of the Enigma": scholars of the ultimate
  "Why"; some younger Baali call them "more Cappadocian than Baali"; their
  covens plan a sacrifice one night, argue Sumerian prophecy the next.
- **The Multitude** — the internecine squabbles that keep the Baali from
  triumphing; "the more energy they expend on internal strife, the less is
  available to… summon the universal demiurge." Occasional betrayal of a
  rival infernal school to witch hunters.

### "Blood and Shadows: The Legacy of Azaneal" (p.36)
**Summary:** Fiction epigraph (a priest bleeding to cast a blood-shadow on a
cave wall). The angellis ater are Lasombra infernalists whose alliance with
Azaneal's twelve childer has been strong for decades. The courts of Philip of
Swabia and Pope Innocent III are "rife with Lasombra manipulators, some of whom
just might be agents of the infernal," and "great Mongada" (an archbishop)
consorts with unearthly beings. **Rules hook:** for Azaneali Baali,
**Obtenebration may replace Daimoinon or Presence**; most are "soulless
vessels"; all are sterile.

### "Apostasy and Absolution" and "Baali and the Infernal" (pp.37-38)
**Summary:** The Baali flee crucifixes and religious texts held by true
believers, though they dismiss "True Faith" as untapped inner potential.
Eldest Baali privately hint at powers beyond the Ba'al, a heresy to say aloud.
They are *not* simply minions of Satan: they worship Anu, Dagon, Hecate, Loki
and others, and the devil-worshipper image serves them as camouflage. Many
(especially recruits) make **infernal investments**, but many just use
corrupted true names and trust their own strength; elders (and followers of
the Road of the Hive) are wary of the price. Mortal pawns are hand-picked from
demonologists and would-be cultists (details deferred to *Dark Ages
Companion*).

### Ghouls — the D'habi (p.39)
**Summary:** Nickname "Hyenas." Descendants of Nergal's priests; each family
was split among the Baali orders, and each member was secretly Blood-
Oathbound to Nergal-Shaitan — which paralyzed the older orders when Shaitan
returned. Since Knossos they have endured harsh servitude as researchers,
test subjects and surrogates (impregnating virgins, devouring sacrificial
victims, serving as vessels for possession). The family is degraded:
disfigured (torture scars, missing digits, disease lesions), only one central
estate remains (Damascus; seven once), most are illiterate, and those who
learn to read must burn out their own tongues. **Rules:** Disciplines
Dominate and Presence (any D'habi who shows knowledge of Daimoinon is killed);
**+2 difficulty on all Willpower rolls**; preferred Road Via Diabolis (Via Hyron
forgotten among the young); hierarchy by age; duties: kidnapping, grave
robbing, assassination, ritual "touch" tasks.

### "The Others" — how the Baali see everyone else (pp.40-41)
Short, insulting, in-character verdicts: Assamites (ancient kin — "Assuri,
Akkadi, Babel"), Brujah ("easy prey"), Cappadocians (brothers, but "the
naked blade of a sword" is the only understanding), Followers of Set (wasted
potential), Gangrel (beneath notice), Lasombra (no different from the
Ravnos), Malkavians (mystery or mundane delusion — "tarry not overlong"),
Nosferatu (hypocrites), Ravnos (tricksters), Salubri ("Gentle Saulot…
Pathetic"), Toreador (decadent), Tremere ("such divine treachery"), Tzimisce
(playing with "fear, fiefdoms and the flesh"), Ventrue (lions with
predators), plus Werewolves ("avoid at all costs"), Mages ("the sorcerers
can glimpse parts of the truth that we alone know"), and Wraiths (a duty
to help them "the rest of the way to Hell"). Note the narrator's anti-Salubri
and anti-Tremere asides read as hostile commentary, not neutral fact.

### "An Exotic Epiphany: Baali and the East" (p.41)
Most Baali "histories" are apocryphal, but they tie the bloodline to the East:
Nergal-called-Shaitan said to have gathered his first followers after a
sojourn with "inhuman masters" on "the Roof of the World"; walking corpses
"hungry for life-essence" reported from eastern deserts; Ashur himself
returning broken from an unrecorded eastern campaign.

### New Traits: Knowledges (pp.41-42)
- **Demonology** — lore of angels, demons, devas and devils; unlike Hearth
  Wisdom, "most of what you 'know' is actually true"; one-to-five-dot
  descriptors Dabbler through Visionary. Possessed by Baali, clergy, cultists,
  mystics, scholars, Tremere, witches. Specialties: Christian, Gnostic, Norse,
  Qaballah, Sufi, Wicca.
- **Plague-Breeding** — breeding and directing disease (poisoned wells, catapulting
  corpses, siege plague, natural-looking poisoning); Visionary can "depopulate
  a kingdom." Possessed by Baali, Nosferatu, brigand captains, sorcerers.
  Specialties: Death, Biological Mass Devastation.

### Roads (pp.42-43)
- **Via Diabolis revisited (pp.42-43):** the "Road of the Devil" is not
  mindless malevolence; it is a path for those who exult in a self-chosen
  demonic epithet. The Baali's "lesser" followers (mockingly called "children")
  are easy to hunt, "an embarrassment to and convenient camouflage for the
  ancient scholars… from whose line they descend — **but that minority is
  growing**." The corruption seems tied to contact with the West; degraded
  neonates call themselves servants of Satan (more than one has claimed to be
  Magog); elders fear extinction "on the pyres it sets for itself."
- **Via Hyron — the Road of the Hive (p.43):** the *original* Baali
  Road; "My tribe and I against our enemies; my brother and I against all
  others." Fights the Beast through unity and tribal support. Loyalty order:
  partners and Blood-Oathbound → nest → same order → the bloodline. **Hierarchy
  of Sin table (Minimum Wrongdoing for a Conviction roll):** 10 arguing with a
  fellow follower; 9 taking an outsider's side against another on the Via Hyron;
  8 working against another's plans on the Road; 7 against a nest-member's plans;
  6 betraying a fellow Baali; 5 betraying a fellow traveler; 4 betraying a
  member of your generation or order; 3 a nest-member; 2 an elder; 1 your Sire or
  a Blood-Oathbound Baali. Azaneal teaches Via Diabolis, so his followers owe
  no loyalty to other Baali — the "most damaging aspect" of his revolt.

### Merits and Flaws (pp.44-45)

### Additional Discipline Powers (pp.46-47) — *reference material; go to the DB*
Daimoinon only. **Glimpse the Gulf Beyond** (six-dot): spend Willpower;
Stamina + Occult (diff 7) vs the target's Willpower (diff 7); leftover successes
escalate from lost Willpower/dice (1-2) to unconsciousness (3), a catatonic hour
(4), and permanent gibbering idiocy unless a Willpower roll succeeds (5+);
Baali are immune. **Open the Way** (ten-dot): only attempted three times, all

Legion** (Daimoinon level three, Obfuscate level two): bind a speaking demon
to answer questions truthfully through the Baali's body (Manipulation +
Leadership, diff 6); a demon can say "I am clanless" or "I am not a
vampire." Query the DB for exact rules text.

## Appendix: To Reign in Hell — Infamous Baali (printed pp. 65-67; DB pp. 66-68)
Intro (p.65): the Baali are "reviled, hunted, and inevitably destroyed" — except
that some rise to power by staying useful or hidden; "to court fame is to court
destruction." Four entries:
- **The Many Faces of Shaitan (p.66):** many have claimed the name; of the horde of
  impostors only one survives. The current claimant believes himself Shaitan, may be
  an artifact of Nergal's machinations, first appeared in Toledo (al-Andalus) as
  Magister of the infernal **Black School** (a "Black College"), taught summoning
  rituals and true names to mortals and Cainites, was destroyed with his students by
  the Baali but escaped (west across the sea, or back to the Fertile Crescent).
- **Cybele (pp.66-67):** a childe of *a* Shaitan (which one "may never be known"),
  Embraced in the Knossos labyrinth; escaped Thera; hated Moloch for betraying her
  sire; helped Rome beat Hannibal, rose in Roman Cainite esteem despite periodic
  bloodlust rampages in her temple (the eunuch galli's public rites alienated
  Cainite society); her rituals **sealed Troile and Moloch in the earth, "where they
  still sleep to this day"**; last seen sailing for Crete. Rumor (unsubstantiated)
  puts her at the Iblii-al-Akbar curse ritual as one of the **Decani**, a servant of
  Namtaru, possibly taking one of his 36 names.
- **Azaneal (p.67):** undisputed ruler of Chorazin, "the first Cainite to galvanize
  the bloodline since Shaitan," imposing, black-eyed, aura of darkness; lacks
  Shaitan's subtlety but has strong will. He tells followers he'll unite the Baali
  and usurp the elders; his real goal is to uncover Chorazin's secrets, believing it
  was meant as a **gate** for eternal darkness, which he doesn't know how to
  complete. He lacks Namtaru's body (agents sent to Crete's labyrinth vanish —
  he suspects Decani guards) and focuses on making fertile childer and subjugating
  Europe's Baali.
- **Ma-ri-ah (pp.67-68):** once-concubine of an Akkadian city-state's king, former
  protégé of **Anaduk the Black** (see *Constantinople by Night*); famously calm; works
  alone; veiled in the Islamic style; hates the children of Arikel (Toreador); serves
  the Child **Anoster, "Lord of Despair"** and has the Decani's favor; now working on
  Byzantium and its relations with the Western Church. Rumors: a traitor who destroyed
  her own sire; a Setite or Tremere agent; or a goddess-made-flesh beyond Cainite
  existence (an Eblaite tablet). "The nail that stands too tall is inevitably hammered
  down."

## Appendix II: Of Interest to Scholars and Chirurgeons (printed p.68; DB p.69)
**"The Sleepers Stir."** Many older orders have left the Levant for Europe or the
East. Because the Baali let too many true names slip into the world, the Children
are closer to waking than at any time since the first tribe; mysterious plagues
boil forth with increasing frequency, which the Baali take as evidence Namtaru is
stirring. Some Baali hide; some seek the oldest, first names to control the Children;
others hasten the waking hoping to be spared. In Europe the young "name themselves
Mephisto." "All true Baali know that the end is coming… very soon." The back cover
(DB p.70) lists the book's contents: the history of "the most hated bloodline," the
Diabolists' methods of corruption, new Merits, Flaws and Daimoinon powers.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Names of power as both cosmology and plot engine.** Names pull the Children toward
  waking (Ch.2, pp.13-14); masking/diluting them is the oldest tradecraft; the
  translation of grimoires and Nergal's seeding of secondary names have degraded the
  bloodline's monopoly (pp.23, 25) and brought Namtaru toward waking (Appendix II).
- **Betrayal as the Baali's defining act.** Moloch betrays Nergal; the D'habi betray
  Nergal; Nergal feigns death; Hay-Tau betrays Shaitan; Assamites confess; elders betray
  young nests to the clans; Azaneal wars on elders; a loyalty Road (Via Hyron) exists
  precisely because loyalty is rare. Loyal unity is repeatedly proposed as the missing
  ingredient (the Multitude, pp.35 and 37; Via Hyron, p.43).
- **Plural, unreliable history.** Chapter Two is explicitly "one and all Baali"; the
  Angra Mainyu fragment is a partisan prophecy; "Ashur the Unnamed" lists mutually
  contradictory origins; third Baali and "Shaitan" identities are unresolved; the Appendix
  repeatedly offers rumors alongside claims. Do not flatten these into one canon.
- **Camouflage and scapegoating.** The "devil-worshipper" image, the Beelzebub
  misidentification, and the "lesser Baali" Satanists give the elders cover; the narrator
  calls the Satanist minority an embarrassment that is growing.
- **Religion as raw material and as vulnerability.** The Baali manipulate Mithraism,
  Christianity, Islam and the Mesopotamian and Egyptian pantheons — and are paralyzed by
  True Faith, which the elders privately read as evidence of powers beyond the Ba'al.
- **Scholarship vs. savagery.** The ancient scholar-philosopher Baali (Celestial, Mystery,
  Hive) contrast with the "lesser Baali" who pile up atrocities; the book treats the
  scholarly ideal as dying.
- **Swarm, plague, organ pit.** A body-horror motif — insect hosts, the Decani, organ pits
  as consecrated ground — runs from the opening fiction to the character templates.
- **Geography of influence.** The Tigris/Euphrates → Phoenicia/Carthage → Rome → Egypt →
  Damascus/Baghdad/Cordoba → Chorazin/Galilee → Europe (Toledo, Valencia, the Frankish
  kingdoms): the bloodline's trail follows empires, trade, translation and Crusade.
